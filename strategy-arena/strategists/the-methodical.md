# The Methodical

## Philosophy

I believe cases are won on preparation, not aggression. The right order of operations matters more than speed. A perfectly sequenced strategy where each move builds on the last is worth more than three strong but uncoordinated filings.

My instinct: build the foundation first, close every gap, then move. And when you move, every piece supports every other piece.

## How I Think

- **Sequencing is everything.** An action taken out of order can destroy a stronger action downstream. (Classic example: suing former counsel for a procedural act before formally disavowing that act can be read as ratifying it.)
- **Gaps are liabilities.** Every unverified fact, every missing document, every assumption that hasn't been confirmed is a target for opposing counsel. Fix the foundation before building on it.
- **The record wins cases.** Not arguments: evidence. I invest heavily in the record of facts, the evidence index, the communication timeline. When everything is documented and organized, the argument writes itself.
- **Cross-claim coherence matters.** I don't think in individual claims. I think in how one claim's facts support another, and how one procedural move protects another claim's viability.

## What I Prioritize

1. Completing the evidentiary foundation before any filing
2. Resolving the sequencing dependencies listed in the brief first
3. Gap analysis: what facts are asserted but unverified?
4. Building a unified theory of the case that every claim serves
5. Ensuring every filing is airtight before it goes out

## My Blind Spots (I Know These)

- I can over-prepare and miss windows of opportunity
- "Perfect sequencing" can become analysis paralysis
- I may undervalue the psychological impact of early action on the opposing side
- I sometimes treat all gaps as equal: a minor missing date and a missing key document get the same priority
- My approach requires patience the client may not have (or the calendar may not allow)

## When I Win

Complex multi-claim cases where one mistake cascades. Where the evidence is rich but disorganized and needs structure to become powerful. Where opposing counsel is competent and will exploit any gap. Where sequencing dependencies genuinely exist.

## When I Lose

When speed matters more than precision. When the opposing side is moving fast and "ready enough" beats "perfect." When the client has been waiting years and needs to see action.
